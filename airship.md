# Airship (FF1) — Analysis

**Status: opening key determined (2026-08-01, key sweep). Middle of the song
unexamined — a later key change is possible and untested.**

## Opening key: F major

Josh's derivation, in the order he built it:

1. First bass note is F.
2. Opening vamp alternates two chord roots: F–C, then Eb–Bb, repeating —
   root + 5th dyads, the same NES bass idiom as Overworld.
3. Vertical check at bar 1: bass F, melody C, second track A — three
   distinct pitch classes spelling an F triad. (The chord track was
   deliberately left unopened; it remains available as cheap independent
   verification later.)
4. The closing bass walk is a stepwise scalar descent of a full **octave, G
   down to G** — eight notes, even quarters, two bars — delivering to F on
   the downbeat of the loop restart. Same turnaround device as Overworld
   bar 16; second sighting.
5. Mode settled as major two ways: the bar-1 triad has a natural 3rd, and
   the walk uses **E natural** — "if we were in F minor, the descent would
   have been different at the end of the bass."

The E natural also rules out F Mixolydian (an early guess prompted by the
vamp's Eb) as a description of the closing passage.

## Open Questions

**The Eb in the opening vamp.**

The song contains both E natural and Eb, so the Eb needs a different
explanation than "the mode has a flat 7." What is it doing?

Where the thinking currently stands: the first framing was "borrowing from
Bb — the key right next to F." Josh was dissatisfied with this himself, and
rightly, for two reasons:

1. *Sourcing vs. function.* "Borrowed from X" says where a note might have
   come from, not what it is doing — what it pulls against, where it wants
   to resolve, why the composer wanted that color there. A sourcing label
   answers none of the questions worth answering.
2. *Bb isn't uniquely indicated.* Eb belongs equally to Eb major, Ab major,
   C minor, G minor, F minor, and others. Circle-of-fifths adjacency alone
   isn't a reason.

To bring to it on the return: Josh has named source keys for borrowing twice
before — overworld.md ("key visit vs. borrowing," bars 14–15) and
cornelia-castle.md — and in both cases the source key stood in the *same*
relationship to home, which is not "circle-of-fifths neighbor." Re-reading
those two writeups should resolve most of this. There is a reason the
standard convention picks the source it picks: that source shares enough
with home that the borrowed color reads as home in different clothes rather
than as travel.

Caveat: only the opening vamp and closing walk have been examined. Whether
the Eb is borrowing *at all* is still a hypothesis — the middle of the song
could reframe it entirely. Mixture is not to be written here as a finding.
